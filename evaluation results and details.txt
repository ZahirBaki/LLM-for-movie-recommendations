Collaborative Filtering Evaluation – Experiment 1

1. Objective

The purpose of this experiment was to evaluate the collaborative filtering
component of the movie recommender system.

Three recommendation approaches were compared:

1. Popularity Baseline
2. Raw Item-Item KNN Collaborative Filtering
3. Mean-Centered Item-Item KNN Collaborative Filtering

The goal was to determine whether collaborative filtering provides better
personalized recommendations than simply recommending popular movies, and
to compare raw ratings against mean-centered ratings.


2. Dataset

Dataset: MovieLens 32M

Original dataset:
- Ratings: 32,000,204
- Users: 200,948
- Movies with ratings: 84,432

The MovieLens ratings were used for the collaborative filtering experiment.


3. Definition of a Relevant Movie

A movie was considered relevant/liked when:

Rating >= 4.0

Ratings below 4.0 were not considered relevant for evaluation, although
they remained available as part of the collaborative filtering training
data.


4. Evaluation Users

Users were required to have at least 10 positive ratings (rating >= 4.0).

Eligible users:
191,556

From these users, 1,000 users were randomly selected for evaluation.

Random state:
42

Using a fixed random state makes the experiment reproducible.


5. Train/Test Split

For each of the 1,000 evaluation users, 20% of their positively rated
movies were randomly hidden.

These hidden ratings formed the test set.

Training ratings:
31,983,686

Hidden positive test ratings:
16,518

The hidden ratings were removed from the training data before the
collaborative models were constructed.

A leakage check confirmed that none of the hidden test rows remained
in the training data.


6. Movie Filtering

Movies were required to have at least 50 ratings in the training data.

Minimum movie ratings:
50

Movies remaining after filtering:
16,033

Ratings remaining after filtering:
31,482,265


7. Recommendation Configuration

Collaborative filtering type:
Item-item collaborative filtering

Algorithm:
K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)

Similarity metric:
Cosine similarity

KNN neighbors retrieved per seed movie:
50

Maximum positive seed movies per user:
10

Final recommendation list:
Top 10 movies


8. Models / Methods Evaluated


A. Popularity Baseline

The popularity baseline did not use personalization or machine learning.

Movies were ranked according to how many ratings they received in the
training data.

For each user, movies they had already rated were removed and the 10 most
popular remaining movies were recommended.


B. Raw KNN Collaborative Filtering

The original MovieLens rating values were used to construct a movie-user
matrix.

KNN with cosine similarity was used to identify movies with similar user
rating patterns.

For each user, up to 10 positively rated movies were used as seed movies.

For each seed movie, 50 nearest movie neighbors were retrieved.

Candidate similarity scores were combined, movies already rated by the
user were removed, and the 10 highest-scoring candidates were recommended.


C. Mean-Centered KNN Collaborative Filtering

This model used the same KNN and cosine-similarity approach.

However, before constructing the movie-user matrix, each user's average
rating was subtracted from their individual ratings.

Example:

User average rating = 3.5

Movie rated 5.0 -> centered rating = +1.5
Movie rated 4.0 -> centered rating = +0.5
Movie rated 3.5 -> centered rating = 0.0
Movie rated 2.0 -> centered rating = -1.5

The purpose of mean-centering was to account for differences between users
who generally give high ratings and users who generally give low ratings.


9. Evaluation Metrics

Two metrics were used:

Recall@10

Recall@10 measures how many of the hidden relevant movies were recovered
within the model's Top-10 recommendations.

Higher Recall@10 is better.


NDCG@10

NDCG@10 measures both whether relevant movies were recovered and where
they appeared in the Top-10 ranking.

Relevant movies appearing near the top of the recommendation list receive
more credit than relevant movies appearing near the bottom.

Higher NDCG@10 is better.

11. Recall@10 Comparison

Raw KNN vs Popularity Baseline:

0.1754 - 0.1086 = 0.0668

Raw KNN achieved approximately 6.68 percentage points higher Recall@10.

Relative improvement:

(0.1754 - 0.1086) / 0.1086 ≈ 61.5%

Therefore, Raw KNN achieved approximately 61.5% higher Recall@10 relative
to the popularity baseline.


Mean-Centered KNN vs Popularity:

0.1454 - 0.1086 = 0.0368

Mean-Centered KNN achieved approximately 3.68 percentage points higher
Recall@10 than the popularity baseline.


Raw KNN vs Mean-Centered KNN:

0.1754 - 0.1454 = 0.0300

Raw KNN achieved approximately 3.00 percentage points higher Recall@10.


12. NDCG@10 Comparison

Popularity Baseline:
0.1751

Mean-Centered KNN:
0.2281

Raw KNN:
0.2583

Raw KNN achieved the highest NDCG@10.

This indicates that Raw KNN not only recovered more hidden relevant movies
than the other approaches, but also produced better rankings of relevant
movies within the Top-10 recommendation lists.


13. Interpretation

Both collaborative filtering approaches outperformed the non-personalized
popularity baseline on Recall@10 and NDCG@10.

This provides evidence that collaborative filtering is learning useful
personalized user-item patterns rather than simply reproducing movie
popularity.

Contrary to the initial expectation that mean-centering might improve the
recommendations, Raw KNN performed better than Mean-Centered KNN on both
evaluation metrics under this experimental setup.

Raw KNN achieved:

Recall@10 = 0.1754
NDCG@10   = 0.2583

Mean-Centered KNN achieved:

Recall@10 = 0.1454
NDCG@10   = 0.2281

Popularity achieved:

Recall@10 = 0.1086
NDCG@10   = 0.1751

Therefore, Raw KNN was the strongest of the three approaches in this
experiment.

These results do not establish that Raw KNN is universally superior to
mean-centered collaborative filtering. They show that Raw KNN performed
better under the dataset, preprocessing, recommendation strategy,
hyperparameters, and evaluation procedure used in this experiment.


14. Current Conclusion

The experiment establishes a useful collaborative filtering baseline.

Current strongest collaborative approach:

Raw Item-Item KNN
+ Cosine Similarity
+ MovieLens user-rating behavior

Current performance:

Recall@10 = 0.1754
NDCG@10   = 0.2583

It outperformed both:

- the popularity baseline
- mean-centered item-item KNN

15. Next Step

The next stage is to determine whether Raw KNN should be adopted directly
as Collaborative Recommender v1 or whether it should first be compared
against another collaborative filtering algorithm, such as matrix
factorization.

After selecting the collaborative approach, the collaborative recommender
can be connected to the existing TMDB content-based recommender to create
the hybrid movie recommendation system.
